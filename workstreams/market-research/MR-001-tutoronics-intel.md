## 2. Tutoronics — What Minahil Observed

### Pricing pain points
Parents raise pricing as a concern sometimes — not the dominant complaint but recurrent. Exact frequency unknown. Pricing sensitivity is real enough to track.

### Tutor payout structure
- Minahil receives **Rs. 2,250/hour** via Filter Tutors
- Filter Tutors connects tutors to Tutoronics and takes commission
- **Filter Tutors takes 30% of Minahil's commission for the first month of any new student** (acquisition fee model, not ongoing)
- Parent-facing rate not directly confirmed — see section 4 for market proxies

### Subject demand
- Demand varies. A/O Level subjects with consistent demand: **Business Studies**, **Physics**
- Economics not specifically mentioned but included in standard A-Level demand (see market data, section 4)

### Student feedback (what students want)
- **More connection and engagement** — students feel the difference when a tutor is genuinely present vs. delivering content mechanically
- Generic lecture or book-reading format is the current norm on the platform
- Students respond to tutors who feel invested in them

### Session format (Tutoronics standard vs. Minahil's approach)

| | Standard on platform | Minahil's approach |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 1 hour | 1 hour |
| Format | Lecture or book reading | Slide deck + quiz at session start + short video or assignment |
| Engagement | Passive delivery | Active: tests what student recalls, sets tasks |

**Observation:** The quiz-at-start and assignment-close is a structured teaching loop not common on the platform. This is a replicable differentiator — it can be required of all Learners' Avenue tutors, not just Minahil.

Filter Tutors charges Minahil 30% of her commission for month one of any new student. This is a referral/acquisition fee.

## 6. Key Market Pain Points (from research)

1. **Billing confusion** — Superprof, Preply both have significant complaint volumes around subscriptions, unexpected charges, and refunds. Parents want simple, transparent pricing.
2. **Tutor quality inconsistency** — marketplace model means wide variance. Parents can't predict what they get.
3. **Board exam ignorance** — tutors who don't know the specific exam code (CAIE vs Edexcel IAL, exact syllabus) miss mark scheme nuance. This is a recurring complaint.
4. **Geography and logistics** — Sharjah parents frequently can't access Dubai-based tutors (90-minute commute during peak hours). Online eliminates this entirely. Learners' Avenue is online-only — this is an advantage, not a limitation.
5. **No ongoing parent communication** — most platforms offer no progress updates. EdFlik's monthly reports are a differentiator they explicitly market.

## 8. Preliminary Signals for SA-2 (Strategy)

These are observations, not recommendations. SA-2 interprets.

- The structured teaching format (quiz-start, deck, assignment-close) is not standard on platforms — it's trainable and scalable as a Learners' Avenue standard
- The $25/session price is defensible against Tutopiya and positions below Spires/premium — but only if curation and cultural fit are clearly communicated
- The billing complaint pattern on Superprof and Preply is a direct acquisition signal: parents who've had bad billing experiences are ready to switch to simple, transparent pricing
- South Asian cultural fit is unaddressed by any of the four agencies reviewed — none explicitly targets Pakistani/Indian expat families in the UAE
- Sharjah is underserved due to the Dubai-commute problem; online-only removes this barrier entirely
