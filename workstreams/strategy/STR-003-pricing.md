## The Product Suite

This is not a tutoring agency with one product. It is a commerce education system with five entry points. A student can engage at any level depending on need and budget.

| Product | What it is | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Enrollment** | One-time onboarding fee. Covers gap analysis, learning plan, dashboard account setup. | All regular students |
| **Standard sessions** | Weekly 1-on-1 tutoring. Structured format: deck + quiz-start + assignment-close. Board-specific (CAIE/Edexcel IAL). | Students wanting ongoing support |
| **Crash course** | 3-hour intensive pre-exam session. One topic or one paper type. No enrollment required. | Students near an exam |
| **Diagnostic session** | 60-minute mentor-led gap analysis. Produces a written learning plan. Standalone purchase. | Students who don't know where they're losing marks |
| **Dashboard access** | Student account with lectures, notes, past papers, quizzes, organised by subject/topic/paper. Included for enrolled students. | Regular students (included) |
| **Module packs** | Standalone topic units. E.g., "Paper 3 Economics — Data Response Technique" or "Business Studies — Finance chapter full pack". 1–2 hours of content + quiz. | Any student, no enrollment required |

## Pricing Architecture — Strategic Logic

**Why an enrollment fee:**
It filters out casual enquiries. A parent who pays $49 to enrol is committed. It also funds the cost of onboarding (Minahil's time for gap analysis and account setup). Not charging for this is leaving money on the table and undervaluing the diagnostic work.

**Why crash courses are priced high ($95 for 3 hours):**
These are sold to parents in crisis. An exam is in 10 days. Their child has not understood Paper 3 Economics. The willingness to pay is high. Tutopiya charges AED 65/hr — Learners' Avenue charges an effective $32/hr for a crash course (same maths). But the framing is completely different: it is not an hourly rate, it is a crisis solution with a price that signals it will work.

**Why modules are cheap ($18 per topic):**
They are the lead generation product. A student who can't afford $38/session can still buy a $18 module. If the quality is there, they convert to enrollment or bring a friend. This is the bottom of the funnel, not the margin driver.

**Why the monthly retainer exists:**
Cash flow. A parent paying $240/month is not going anywhere. Minahil can plan. Tutors can be scheduled reliably. The alternative (per-session booking) creates admin chaos and unpredictable revenue.

**Why founding pricing is permanent (not a trial):**
Trial discounts expire and create resentment. Founding rates are a reward for early trust. The first 20 students tell the next 50 — their loyalty is the acquisition channel. Resenting them when their discount expires kills that.
